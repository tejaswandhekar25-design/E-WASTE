🗂️ Segregation Hierarchy

The Four E-Waste Segregation Streams

Scientific e-waste management classifies discarded electronics into four distinct channels to maximize resource recovery while eliminating environmental hazards.

🟢
Stream 1: Highest Value

Reusable Electronics

Electronics in working condition that have reached end-of-use for their first owner, but retain full functional utility for schools, students, or NGOs.

  • ✓ Working laptops, desktops, and monitors
  • ✓ Operational tablets and smartphones
  • ✓ Functional routers, power adapters, and keyboards
  • Action: Secure data wipe, cosmetic cleaning, and donation or second-hand resale.
🔵
Stream 2: Circular Economy

Repairable & Upcyclable

Devices with broken components (e.g. dead battery, damaged port, broken glass) that can be restored with modular spare parts.

  • ✓ Laptops needing RAM/SSD upgrades or battery swaps
  • ✓ Phones with screen or charging port failure
  • ✓ Printers requiring roller or gear replacement
  • Action: Diagnostic testing, component refurbishing, and warranty repair.
🟣
Stream 3: Urban Mining

Recyclable Materials

Non-functional units dismantled into base metals, printed circuit boards (PCBs), engineering plastics, and wiring for smelting and refining.

  • ✓ High-grade motherboards & gold-plated pin headers
  • ✓ Copper motor windings, heat pipes, and AC cords
  • ✓ Aluminum chassis and steel bracket shielding
  • Action: Mechanical shredding, eddy-current separation, and hydrometallurgical recovery.
🔴
Stream 4: High Risk

Hazardous Components

Toxic materials requiring specialized hermetic isolation, thermal destruction, or hazardous waste disposal facilities to prevent soil poisoning.

  • ✓ Swollen Lithium-ion polymer cells (fire hazard)
  • ✓ Mercury-containing cold-cathode fluorescent lamps (CCFLs)
  • ✓ Lead-acid battery electrolyte & CRT leaded funnel glass
  • Action: Isolation in non-conductive containers, certified hazardous e-waste handlers.

⛏️ Recoverable Raw Materials Breakdown

Typical material composition found in common electronics that can be recovered through authorized industrial recycling channels:

Device Category Ferrous Metals (Steel/Iron) Non-Ferrous (Copper/Aluminium) Precious Metals (Gold/Silver) Plastics & Polymers Hazardous Constituents
💻 Laptops & PCs ~20% (chassis, screws) ~14% (heatsinks, wire) ~0.02% (Gold pins, contacts) ~25% (ABS / Polycarbonate) Lead solder, Li-ion cells
📱 Smartphones ~5% (micro-screws) ~15% (antennas, inductors) ~0.035% (Gold, Silver, Palladium) ~40% (casing, substrates) Cobalt, Lithium, Arsenic traces
🖨️ Printers & Scanners ~45% (heavy steel frames) ~8% (stepper motors, copper) Traces (controller boards) ~40% (plastic outer shell) Toner micro-powders, Ozone filters
🔌 Cables & Chargers ~5% (plugs) ~45% (Pure Copper wire) None ~45% (PVC insulation) Brominated flame retardants (BFRs)

*Figures represent generalized industry benchmarks (UN Global E-waste Monitor & StEP Initiative). Real recovery yields depend on recycling equipment efficiency and feed purity.

Why Mixing E-Waste with Regular Trash is Catastrophic

💧 Groundwater Contamination

When circuit boards and batteries are dumped in landfills, acid rain dissolves toxic lead, cadmium, and arsenic, creating toxic leachate that poisons drinking aquifers.

🔥 Landfill Explosions & Fires

Damaged lithium-ion batteries easily undergo thermal runaway when crushed by garbage compactors, igniting underground landfill fires that release dioxins and furan fumes.

⛏️ Irreversible Loss of Finite Metals

Throwing away 1 metric ton of smartphones wastes 300 times more gold than is extracted from 1 metric ton of raw gold ore mined from the earth.
